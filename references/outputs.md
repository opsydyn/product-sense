# Output contracts

Choose one format. Adapt length to the request. Use concrete prose and small comparison tables. Put uncertainty beside the claim. Keep author names out of routine outputs unless attribution is requested.

## Product critique

Lead with verdict and decisive reason. Then cover:

1. User progress and current alternative.
2. Supporting evidence and remaining assumptions.
3. Main opportunity, risk or design failure.
4. Better route or scope to cut.
5. Next action, observable outcome and stop/reshape condition.

Combine into five lines for a quick request. Include strategy if fit drives the call. Do not print twelve sections by default.

## Product brief

Write a concise decision document:

- Recommendation: verdict, confidence and why now.
- User and progress: segment, situation, first success and wider goal.
- Reality and evidence: alternative, switch forces, facts, assumptions and sources.
- Strategy and positioning: diagnosis, policy, coherent actions, tradeoff, distinct value and best-fit customer.
- Opportunity and options: need, credible routes, choice and reason.
- Outcome: primary measure, business link, baseline status, proposed target, window and guardrail.
- Risk and learning: critical assumption, credible test, proposed pass/fail/inconclusive interpretation and falsifying observation.
- Boundaries and action: appetite, core scope, exclusions, dependency, owner or required role and next step.

Use unknown and a next evidence step instead of plausible numbers.

## Shaped bet

Use problem, appetite, solution outline, rabbit holes and exclusions. Add user outcome, unresolved evidence, operator responsibilities and completion/stop conditions. State whether this ends in learning, delivery or a pilot. Keep promised capability explicit.

## Design review

Lead with the largest obstacle. Use a short table when useful:

| Step | Expected / actual | User cost | Evidence | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank blocker, friction or polish. Close with the unassisted task, outcome measure and result that changes the fix. State limits of the artifact; unseen states are not observations.

## Final self-check

Confirm that the recommendation concerns user progress and a viable business or service outcome; the strongest claim has evidence or an assumption label; the test addresses the main uncertainty; a plausible failure changes the plan; scope respects fixed constraints and preserves recovery and access; and the response ends with a concrete action.
