# Discovery and product judgment

## Frame progress

Write: “When [situation], [user] wants to [progress], so they can [wider outcome]. Today they [alternative], which costs [supported cost].” Mark gaps as assumptions.

Identify functional, emotional and social needs where supported. Seek first meaningful success, obstacles to repeat success and skills that grow. Remove tool overhead so effort goes toward the domain. Reliable delegation can itself improve capability; not every utility must teach.

Connect one desired outcome to user needs, pain or desires, then to solutions and tests. An opportunity is “I cannot tell which car fits my family,” not “I need a chatbot.” Compare options against the same task. Include today's route and a manual or no-build route when the choice is open.

Diagnose the main obstacle, choose a policy, and identify reinforcing actions. State the tradeoff. Link user progress to a credible business mechanism; benefit alone does not prove a business case.

## Gather evidence

Ask about the last attempt: trigger, steps, tools, problems, consequences and what happened next. Seek artifacts, workarounds, past spending and real commitments. Hear the story before explaining the idea. Record contrary cases.

For switching, seek the event pushing change, attractive progress, fear of the new way and comfort of the old. For positioning, identify what the buyer would really use otherwise, then connect distinct capability to customer value. Competitor feature absence does not establish a moat.

## Make learning decision-ready

Write the critical assumption in falsifiable terms. Match evidence to uncertainty:

| Uncertainty | Useful evidence | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Problem and demand | Recent struggle, costly workaround, real commitment | Usability at scale |
| Comprehension and task success | Unassisted realistic prototype task | Demand or production reliability |
| Feasibility | Focused technical probe | Adoption or business value |
| Value in use | Bounded pilot or suitable comparison | Causality without a sound comparison |

State participant/context, method, observable result, proposed threshold, time limit and action for pass, fail or inconclusive evidence. A small qualitative study finds patterns, not population conversion rates. A payment does not prove retention.

Define the primary behavior measure, baseline availability, window and guardrail. Count those exposed to the flow as well as completions. If evidence is absent, choose a reversible learning bet and explain why its cost is justified.
